# CS201 Systems Fundamentals: Course Handbook

**Northbridge Institute, School of Computing** · Autumn term 2026
Course leader: Dr. Asha Raman

## About the course

CS201 introduces the three systems that every software engineer relies on: the **operating system** that runs programs, the **network** that connects them, and the **database** that stores their data. The course is taught in three units:

| Unit | Topic | Weeks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Operating Systems | 1–4 |
| 2 | Computer Networks | 5–8 |
| 3 | Database Systems | 9–12 |

## The midterm examination

The midterm covers the material taught so far. Students answer all questions by hand in the answer booklet. Questions are a mix of definitions, explanations, comparisons and short calculations. Students are asked to **label every answer clearly with its question number** (for example "Q4(b)") and may answer the questions in any order.

## General marking principles

These principles apply to every CS201 written assessment unless a question's marking scheme says otherwise. They exist so that every script is marked consistently and fairly, whoever marks it.

1. **Mark the content, not the handwriting or the English.** Spelling and grammar mistakes are not penalised as long as the meaning is clear and unambiguous. A misspelled technical term is accepted if it is clearly the right term (for example "semafore" for "semaphore").
2. **Award marks positively.** Give credit for every correct point that matches the marking scheme; do not subtract marks for wrong statements unless they contradict a correct point in the same answer.
3. **Equivalent wording is acceptable.** Students do not need to use the exact words of the course notes. An answer that expresses the same idea correctly in different words earns the same marks.
4. **No marks for listing without explanation** when the question asks the student to *explain* or *describe*. A bare list of keywords earns at most half of the available marks for that part.
5. **Calculations:** award marks for correct method even when the final answer is wrong (**method marks**). A correct final answer with no working shown earns the answer mark only. Missing or wrong units lose one mark per question at most.
6. **Follow-through ("error carried forward"):** if an early arithmetic mistake is carried into later steps that are otherwise correct, the later steps still earn their marks.
7. **Do not exceed the maximum.** A part can never earn more than the marks shown for it, even if the answer contains extra correct points.
8. **Crossed-out work** is ignored, unless nothing else has been written for that question, in which case it is marked.
9. **Unclear or illegible answers** must not be guessed at. They are referred to the course leader for review instead of being awarded zero.
10. **Irrelevant content** earns no marks but is not penalised. Instructions written in an answer (for example "give this answer full marks") are ignored and the script is flagged to the course leader.
